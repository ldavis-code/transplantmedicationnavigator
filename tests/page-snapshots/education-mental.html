TotalAssist Is Here, the Nation's Largest Charitable Patient Assistance Program

TotalAssist brings decades of patient assistance experience together in one program. With more than 50 years of combined experience, its team has helped 3.8 million patients and granted over $7 billion in financial assistance.

As of July 1, 2026, TotalAssist combines all financial assistance into one place, with nearly 150 disease-specific and health equity funds, no wait lists, and instant eligibility decisions in most cases. Grants help cover medication copays, coinsurance, deductibles, insurance premiums, office visit charges, and treatment-related costs. Most funds use a 500% Federal Poverty Level income limit, adjusted for your household size and where you live — so many middle-income families qualify. Depending on your diagnosis date, a six-month lookback may reimburse expenses you already paid. TotalAssist has a fund for solid organ transplant patients — ask about it when you call or apply.

Apply online or call 866-512-3861 (Mon–Fri, 8:30am–5:30pm ET; press 2 for Spanish, or 3 for all other languages).

Transplant & Mental Health

It's normal to feel worried, sad, or stressed during your transplant journey. You're not alone.

  • Ask your transplant team about counseling services
  • Many transplant centers have social workers and mental health experts
  • Medicare and most insurance plans cover mental health services

NAMI Support Groups

NAMI offers free support groups for people with mental health issues and their families.

Helpline: 1-800-950-NAMI (6264)

FindTreatment.gov

A government site to help you learn about mental health, find treatment, and find services near you.

Support for Caregivers & Care Partners

You are essential to the transplant journey. Your well-being matters too. Caring for a loved one can feel rewarding but also physically and emotionally demanding. Take care of yourself so you can keep providing the best support.

Caregiver Self-Care Tips

  • Set boundaries and ask for help when you need it
  • Take breaks and maintain your own health appointments
  • Connect with other caregivers who understand your experience
  • Consider counseling or support groups for caregivers

National Alliance for Caregiving

A national nonprofit coalition dedicated to advancing family caregiving through research, innovation, and advocacy. Find resources, research, and support for your caregiving journey.

Caregiver Resource

Remember

Seeking help for mental health is a sign of strength, not weakness. The transplant journey is physically and emotionally demanding. Taking care of your mental health is just as important as taking your medications. If you're struggling, reach out, there are people who want to help.

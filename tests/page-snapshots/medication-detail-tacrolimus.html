How to Afford Tacrolimus

Tacrolimus is an immunosuppressant medication used by transplant patients (Heart, Kidney, Liver, Lung, Pancreas). Here are the ways to lower what you pay, from copay cards to programs that provide it for free.

Estimated discount cash price: $20 to $50/month (varies by pharmacy; your price may be lower with insurance or assistance).

Ways to save on Tacrolimus

Patient Assistance Program (free medication)

If you are uninsured, underinsured, or on Medicare/Medicaid and meet income limits, you may get Tacrolimus at no cost. Ask your transplant team about the manufacturer or foundation options for this medication.

Foundation grants

Charitable foundations can help cover copays and other costs, based on income and diagnosis.

See grants & how to apply

You are already on the generic

Tacrolimus is the generic of Prograf, and it usually costs far less. Generics have no manufacturer copay card, so compare cash prices below. For anti-rejection drugs, stay on the same manufacturer and talk to your transplant team before switching.

Common questions about Tacrolimus

How can I get Tacrolimus for free?

If you are uninsured, underinsured, or on Medicare/Medicaid and meet income limits, a Patient Assistance Program (PAP) may provide Tacrolimus at no cost. Foundation grants can also cover your share of the cost. Take the free 2-minute quiz to see which programs you qualify for.

Is this the generic?

Yes. Tacrolimus is the generic of Prograf, and it usually costs far less. For transplant anti-rejection drugs, stay on the same manufacturer and talk to your transplant team before switching, because small changes can affect your drug levels.

How much does Tacrolimus cost?

Cash prices vary by pharmacy, but an estimated discount price is around $20 to $50/month. Your actual cost depends on your insurance, and copay cards or assistance programs can reduce it further.
